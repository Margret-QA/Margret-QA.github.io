01 / BUSINESS CONTEXT

From hindsight to foresight

Predictive analytics estimates what might happen next. It is not mind-reading: it makes probabilistic forecasts from observable data.

06 / MEASUREMENT

What does success look like?

Classification

Precision, recall, F1 and ROC-AUC

Probability quality

Calibration and log loss

Demand forecasting

MAE, RMSE and WAPE

Business impact

Incremental conversion, margin and retention

Important: If only 2% of customers buy, a model that always predicts “no” is 98% accurate—but is not useful for targeting.

04 — From data to machine learning

Technical workflow
01 · CollectTransactions, browsing, cart, feedback, permitted context
02 · EngineerRecency, frequency, spending, engagement, price sensitivity
03 · TrainLogistic regression baseline; compare random forest / XGBoost
04 · ValidateTime-based holdout, leakage checks, calibration and error analysis
05 · DeployScore eligible customers and choose relevant offers
06 · LearnA/B-test actions, monitor drift, retrain when appropriate
Precise target variable: Did this customer purchase running shoes in the seven days after the observation date? 1 = Yes, 0 = No. Use only features available at the observation date. Split train/validation/test data chronologically to reduce leakage.

06 — Evaluation scorecard

Model + business KPIs
PrecisionOf predicted buyers, how many actually bought?
RecallOf all buyers, how many did we identify?
ROC-AUCCan the model rank buyers above non-buyers?
Log lossHow well do probability estimates fit outcomes?
CalibrationDo predicted 80% groups buy about 80% of the time?
Incremental conversionDid targeting cause more purchases than a control?
Marketing ROIDoes incremental profit exceed the total investment?
Model driftAre preferences or data patterns changing over time?

When only 2% of customers buy, predicting “no” for everyone is 98% accurate but useless for identifying buyers. Evaluate against a baseline and a time-based test set.
