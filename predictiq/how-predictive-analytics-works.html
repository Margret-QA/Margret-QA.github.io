Stage 01 of 05

Collect: Capture the customer signals

The business question

Will this customer purchase running shoes within the next seven days? The observation time is Monday 10:00. We capture only events available up to that moment.

Example record

Customer RS-1042 • Snapshot Monday 10:00 • Label not yet known. A purchase on Wednesday would make the seven-day outcome 1; no purchase by next Monday would make it 0.

What RunSmart collects

  • Purchase history5 previous orders; last order 45 days ago; average order €85
  • Browsing12 product views; 8-minute session; search “marathon shoes”
  • Engagement3 recent email opens; 2 product clicks; 2 wishlist items
  • Cart & priceRunning shoes added, then abandoned; 60% of past orders discounted
  • Context & feedbackMobile session, evening shopping pattern, prior rating 4.5/5
Managerial insight: Use a lawful basis, clear purposes, minimum necessary fields and access controls. Browsing interest alone is not proof of intent.

Stage 02 of 05

Prepare: Turn raw events into reliable model features

Why preparation matters

Raw clicks and order rows are not directly ready for prediction. Standardize records, remove duplicates, resolve timestamps, and create meaningful features from the observation window.

Example transformation

Events: 12 product-view rows + 1 abandoned-cart event + 5 order records → one feature row for RS-1042 at Monday 10:00. Missing values are flagged rather than silently replaced with zero.

Feature engineering

  • recency_days45 = days since the most recent completed order
  • order_count_365d5 = orders in the prior 365 days
  • product_views_7d12 = running-shoe page views in the previous week
  • session_minutes8 = time spent in the observed session
  • cart_abandoned_7d1 = cart abandonment occurred in the previous week
  • discount_share0.60 = 60% of past orders used discounts
Managerial insight: Prevent leakage: do not include Wednesday's eventual purchase, future emails, or later cart activity in Monday's input.

Stage 03 of 05

Predict: Train, validate and interpret a model

What the model actually learns

Historical customer snapshots are paired with seven-day purchase outcomes. Start with logistic regression as an explainable baseline; compare with random forest or XGBoost if validation improves.

Example output

For the RunSmart demonstration, a model might return a score such as 0.82. This is an illustrative value only. A real 82% probability requires calibration testing: among customers scored near 0.82, approximately 82% should buy within seven days.

Model workflow

  • Training targetPurchased running shoes in the next 7 days: yes (1) or no (0)
  • Data splitEarlier periods for training; later periods for validation and test
  • BaselineLogistic regression estimates purchase propensity from engineered features
  • AlternativesRandom forest or XGBoost can capture nonlinear feature interactions
  • Quality checksPrecision, recall, ROC-AUC, log loss and probability calibration
  • Fairness checksInspect performance differences and unintended proxy effects
Managerial insight: Compare against simple rules and avoid choosing a complex model merely because it sounds more advanced.

04
Stage 04 of 05

Act: Translate the prediction into a decision

Prediction is not the same as action

RunSmart must decide whether to recommend shoes, show a comparison, send a reminder or offer a discount. The most likely buyer is not always the customer who benefits most from a promotion.

Example action

RS-1042 sees a personalized running-shoe comparison and a non-discount cart reminder. RunSmart does not automatically issue a 15% coupon simply because the score is high.

Decision options

  • Relevant product rowShow running shoes matching the customer's browsing preferences
  • Helpful comparisonExplain cushioning, fit, price and customer reviews
  • Cart reminderRemind only where communication permissions allow
  • Discount strategyTest whether a discount causes incremental purchases, rather than rewarding buyers who would purchase anyway
  • InventoryCheck stock and delivery promises before recommending a size
  • Control groupRandomly hold out comparable eligible customers to measure real uplift
Managerial insight: Optimize expected incremental contribution, not clicks or predicted conversion alone. Respect customer choices and avoid intrusive targeting.

Stage 05 of 05

Learn: Measure business value and improve

Close the feedback loop

Record what action was shown, whether a purchase happened, and the costs and margins. Use randomized experiments to distinguish correlation from causal impact; monitor changes in customer preferences.

Example learning

The assumed campaigns differ by €4,200 in contribution after marketing (€7,200 − €3,000), but this does not prove AI caused the improvement. A randomized test is needed because the targeted customers may have bought anyway.

Evaluation and monitoring

  • Model performancePrecision, recall, ROC-AUC, calibration and log loss on future data
  • Experiment designRandomly assign eligible customers to treatment and control
  • Business KPIsIncremental orders, contribution margin, marketing cost and ROI
  • Illustrative comparisonTraditional: 600 orders, €12,000 cost, €3,000 contribution after marketing
  • Illustrative comparisonAI targeted: 480 orders, €4,800 cost, €7,200 contribution after marketing
  • OperationsMonitor drift, data freshness, fairness, privacy and retraining triggers
Managerial insight: Scale only if incremental profit and customer outcomes improve, with monitoring and clear stop criteria.
